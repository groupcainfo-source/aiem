Choosing the right legal form

The legal form decides who is liable, how the company can raise money and how much administration it carries. For most small and medium businesses the choice is between a limited liability company (SH.P.K.) and, for larger or more complex projects, a joint stock company (SH.A.). Other options include an individual business and a branch of a foreign company. A branch is part of its parent and does not limit the parent's liability, which is why many investors prefer a separate local company.

  • SH.P.K. A common choice for owners who want limited liability and a simple structure.
  • SH.A. Suited to projects with many shareholders, or plans to raise capital by issuing shares.
  • Individual business. Simple, but the owner is personally responsible for the business.
  • Branch of a foreign company. Useful when the parent wants to operate directly, with the liability consequences described above.

The registration process step by step

  1. Decide the form, the name, the object of activity and the share structure.
  2. Prepare the founding documents: the statute and the founding act, with the details of owners and administrators.
  3. Provide identification of the owners and administrators. For foreigners, documents issued abroad usually need an apostille or legalisation and a certified translation.
  4. Register with the National Business Centre (QKB). On registration the company receives its business identification number (NUIS).
  5. Open a bank account for the company and deposit the share capital, if required for the chosen form.
  6. Complete registration for taxes and, if the company will employ people, for employer obligations and social insurance.
  7. Set up accounting and a calendar of recurring filings and payments.

Questions to settle before you register

  • Object of activity. It must match what you will really do, because some activities need a licence or permit before you start.
  • Registered address. The company needs a seat in Albania, and the address should be one you can use to receive official letters.
  • Owners and control. Albanian rules require the real beneficial owners to be identified, so the structure must be transparent from the beginning.
  • Administrator. Who will manage the company, and whether that person needs to live in Albania.
  • Taxes. Whether and when the company must register for value added tax depends on its activity and expected turnover, and should be confirmed with an accountant.

Registering from abroad

Many foreign founders ask whether they must travel to Albania. In some cases the process can be completed with a power of attorney given to a representative. This requires the document to be prepared properly, signed in front of the right authority and legalised, which takes time if it is not planned. We tell you at the start what is possible in your case and which documents you will need to prepare in your country.

After registration: the obligations people forget

  • Regular tax filings and payments, which have fixed dates.
  • Annual financial statements.
  • Keeping the company register up to date when owners, addresses or administrators change.
  • Employment contracts and social insurance for staff.
  • Licences and permits linked to the activity, which may need renewal.

A good accountant who knows your activity pays for themselves by avoiding late filings and penalties. We can introduce partner accountants, and we connect company formation with the business plan and, where relevant, with property purchase so that the legal structure fits the investment.

A realistic timeline

Once the documents are complete, the registration step itself can be quick. The delays usually come from elsewhere: documents issued abroad that need an apostille and a certified translation, the bank's onboarding and compliance checks for a new company, and licences for regulated activities. We give you a calendar at the start that separates the steps you control from the ones you do not, so that a launch date is based on the slowest step, not the fastest.

What we need from you to start

  • What the company will do, in two or three sentences.
  • The names, nationalities and ownership shares of the owners.
  • Who will administer the company.
  • Whether you will be present in Albania or need to act through a representative.
  • Your expected start date.

Frequently asked questions

Can a foreigner set up a company in Albania?

In general yes. Conditions depend on the form and the activity. We verify the current requirements before you start.

Do I need to be present in Albania?

In some cases the process can be completed with a power of attorney. We confirm it for your case.

How long does registration take?

Registration itself is fast, but the total time depends on documents and the bank. We give you a realistic calendar at the start.

What is the difference between SH.P.K. and SH.A.?

An SH.P.K. is the usual form for small and medium companies with a small number of owners. An SH.A. is designed for larger structures and for raising capital through shares. We recommend a form after learning about your plans.

Can I keep my ownership private?

Albanian rules require beneficial owners to be identified and registered, so the structure must be transparent. We explain what information becomes available and to whom.

Do I need an accountant?

In practice yes. Even a small company has recurring filings and payments, and mistakes lead to penalties. We can introduce partner accountants.

Can you help after the company is registered?

Yes. We can support with the bank account, the first contracts, partners and the business plan, and put you in touch with accountants and lawyers.
