Why electrical design deserves its own engineering work

An electrical installation is the one system in a building that every other system depends on, and also the one most often improvised on site. A cable that is slightly too small, a breaker that is not coordinated with the one upstream, or an earthing system that was never measured can work for years and then fail at the worst moment. Designing the installation first is cheaper than correcting it later.

What a complete electrical project covers

  • Load calculation for every circuit and for the building as a whole, with allowance for growth.
  • Cable sizing for current, voltage drop and fault conditions.
  • Short-circuit levels and protection coordination, so that a fault trips the nearest breaker and not the whole site.
  • Distribution switchboards with clear single-line diagrams and labelling.
  • Earthing and equipotential bonding, and lightning protection where the building requires it.
  • Lighting design that meets the task and keeps energy use low.
  • Automation and control, including PLC systems for machinery and building services.

Power factor, harmonics and the bill you actually pay

Many industrial customers pay more than necessary because of poor power factor or because electronic loads distort the supply. Motors, welders and drives can draw reactive power that the utility may charge for. Variable-speed drives and rectifiers create harmonics that heat cables and transformers and disturb sensitive equipment. We measure these effects, size power-factor correction where it makes sense and check whether filters are needed, before recommending any expensive equipment.

Solar photovoltaic systems: sizing for self-consumption

A photovoltaic system is worth the most when it supplies electricity that you would otherwise buy during the day. We therefore start from your consumption profile, not from the roof size. The study compares system sizes, shows how much of the production you would use yourself, estimates payback under clearly stated assumptions and checks the roof structure, shading, cable routes and the point of connection. Grid connection follows the procedures of the distribution operator, and we prepare the technical documentation that the process requires. We do not promise a specific saving, because it depends on tariffs, consumption and the rules in force at the time.

Automation that operators can actually maintain

Good automation is simple enough for your own technicians to diagnose. We specify controllers, sensors and drives that are available in the region, document the logic in plain language and design the control panels with accessible test points. If a supplier will program the machinery, we write a clear functional specification so that the result matches what the process needs.

Measurements and tests before commissioning

Before an installation goes live, it should be tested and the results recorded. Depending on the project this includes insulation resistance, continuity, earth-loop impedance, earth-electrode resistance, residual-current device trip times and correct phase sequence. For existing sites we can also use thermal imaging on switchboards to find loose connections before they fail. You receive the protocols, which are also what an insurer or inspector will ask to see.

What we need from you to start

  • Building plans or a description of the site and its main loads.
  • Recent electricity bills and, if available, the load profile.
  • A list of machinery with power ratings and nameplate data.
  • Existing electrical drawings, even if they are outdated.
  • Any plans for expansion in the next three to five years.

Common problems we correct

  • Switchboards that are full, unlabelled and impossible to extend.
  • Cables loaded beyond their rating after several machines were added.
  • No selectivity, so one short circuit blacks out a whole building.
  • Earthing that was installed but never measured.
  • Photovoltaic systems sized for the roof instead of for the load.

Electrical design usually goes hand in hand with mechanical design, because motors, heating and cooling are the largest electrical loads, and the investment case for a plant or a solar system can be written into a business plan. For a new building, we coordinate both disciplines so that the cable routes and the plant rooms do not collide.

We design to recognised international and European installation standards and to the requirements of the Albanian authorities and the grid operator that apply to your project. Final approvals remain with the competent authorities.

Frequently asked questions

Do you design solar panel installations?

Yes. We assess consumption, system size and payback. Grid connection follows the procedures of the distribution operator.

What documents do I receive at the end?

Single-line diagrams, a materials list, measurement and test protocols, and maintenance instructions.

Do you assess energy consumption?

Yes. We analyse consumption and propose measures with approximate cost and payback time.

Can you design the installation for a new factory from scratch?

Yes. We start from the process and the machinery list, calculate the loads, design the distribution and produce the drawings and specifications for tender and construction.

Do you test existing installations?

Yes. We measure and inspect existing installations, report the findings in order of risk and propose corrections with an estimated cost.

How do I know whether solar panels make sense for my business?

We compare your hourly or daily consumption with the expected production of different system sizes, then show how much you would consume yourself and what the payback is under stated assumptions.

Can you help reduce my electricity bill?

Often yes. We look at tariff structure, power factor, peak demand and wasted consumption, and rank the measures by payback.
