Why most market entries are really partner decisions

A company entering a new country rarely fails because the product is bad. It fails because the first local partner was chosen quickly, on the basis of a good meeting and a friendly introduction. In a smaller market the effect is larger, because there are fewer partners to choose from and a bad fit is harder to replace. Our work is to make that choice deliberate.

The routes into the Albanian market

Before looking for a partner, it helps to decide which route you want. Each has a different cost, a different level of control and a different risk.

  • Distributor. A local company buys from you and resells. Fast and inexpensive to start, but you have less control over price and brand.
  • Sales agent. A local person or company finds customers and earns a commission, while you invoice directly. More control, but you carry the stock and the credit risk.
  • Joint venture. You and a local company set up a shared business. Combines your product with their knowledge and relationships, and needs a carefully drafted agreement.
  • Own subsidiary or branch. Maximum control, with the full cost of setting up and running it. Often a later step once demand is proven.
  • Contract manufacturing or local assembly. Used when transport costs, tariffs or lead times make local production attractive.

How we find and screen partners

  1. Brief. Your product, your target customers, the volumes you hope for and what you want from a partner.
  2. Market study. Who buys, through which channels, and who already sells similar products.
  3. Long list. Companies that appear to fit, from our network and from open sources.
  4. Screening. We check what the company really does, its customers, its financial standing as far as it can be verified and its reputation with suppliers and buyers.
  5. Short list and meetings. We prepare the introductions, interpret between business cultures where needed and take notes.
  6. Terms. We help you structure the relationship and prepare the points for the contract.

What a good partnership agreement covers

  • Territory and whether it is exclusive, and what happens if the partner misses its targets.
  • Minimum purchase or sales commitments, and how they are reviewed.
  • Pricing, discounts, payment terms and who carries the credit risk.
  • Use of your brand, trademarks and marketing material.
  • Service, warranty and spare-parts responsibilities.
  • Duration, renewal and clear termination rules, including stock and customer handover.
  • Confidentiality and protection of your know-how.

Contracts are drafted and reviewed by licensed lawyers. We prepare the commercial points and work with partner lawyers so that the agreement matches what was agreed in the room.

Culture and communication

Business in Albania often moves through personal relationships and direct conversation. Foreign companies used to formal procedures can read that as disorder, while local partners can read formality as distrust. Having someone who understands both sides, and who can translate not only words but expectations, saves time and avoids misunderstandings that later look like disputes.

Signs of a good partner, and of a bad one

  • Good: it already sells to the customers you want, and can show it.
  • Good: it is willing to commit to targets and to put them in the contract.
  • Good: its own suppliers and customers speak well of it.
  • Bad: it wants exclusivity for the whole country without any commitment to sell.
  • Bad: it asks for large advance payments or for credit that you cannot control.
  • Bad: it cannot explain how it would reach your customers.

Frequently asked questions

Which industries do you work in?

Any industry. For each project we work with experts from the relevant field.

Do you represent foreign companies in Albania?

We help you find a local partner and organise the process. Legal representation is handled through the appropriate legal structures.

How is the service paid?

A fixed fee for the study phase and, where it makes sense, a success structure agreed in writing before we start.

Do you act as my agent or distributor in Albania?

No. We help you find, check and meet suitable partners and structure the relationship. The partner becomes your counterparty, not us.

Can you help a company that already has a partner but is unhappy?

Yes. We can review the relationship, identify what is not working and help you renegotiate or look for an alternative.

How long does it take to find a suitable partner?

It depends on the industry and the requirements. A first short list is usually possible after the market study, and meetings follow. We give you a written timeline at the start.

Is the service only for foreign companies?

No. Albanian companies also use it to find foreign suppliers, technology partners and distributors.
