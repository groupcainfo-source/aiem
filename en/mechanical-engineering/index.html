Signs your mechanical systems need engineering attention

Many factories and commercial buildings run on systems that were assembled piece by piece over the years: a new machine here, an extra fan there, a boiler replaced when it failed. Nothing is obviously wrong until the costs show up. If any of the following sounds familiar, a structured mechanical review will usually pay for itself.

  • One production line stops several times a month for reasons nobody can fully explain.
  • Energy bills keep rising while output stays the same.
  • Ventilation cannot keep heat, dust, fumes or humidity within acceptable limits.
  • New equipment was bought before anyone checked that power, compressed air, water or steam could support it.
  • The drawings no longer match what is actually installed, so every repair starts with guesswork.

How we run a mechanical project

We work in stages, so that decisions rest on evidence and can be reviewed before money is spent on equipment.

  1. Brief and site survey. We agree what must be produced, how much, and under which conditions. We measure the site and the existing equipment instead of assuming.
  2. Concept. Two or three layout options with utility needs and a rough cost range, so you can choose a direction early.
  3. Basic design. Heat loads, air flows, pipe and duct sizes, support structures and equipment specifications, all backed by calculations you can inspect.
  4. Supplier comparison. A technical table that compares offers on the same basis, not only on price.
  5. Detailed design. Drawings, schedules and instructions that a contractor can build from without guessing.
  6. Supervision and commissioning. We check the installation against the drawings, witness the tests and hand over complete documentation.

Calculations that prevent expensive mistakes

Oversizing is one of the most common and least visible errors. A heating, ventilation or cooling system sized with a large safety margin costs more to buy, runs inefficiently at part load and often has control problems. Undersizing is just as costly, because the plant cannot hold the conditions the process needs. We size systems from calculated loads rather than from rules of thumb:

  • Heat gains and losses through the building envelope, equipment, lighting and people.
  • Ventilation air for occupants and for process emissions such as dust, solvents or steam.
  • Pressure drops in pipes and ducts, which decide the pumps and fans you actually need.
  • Compressed-air demand, including a realistic allowance for leaks.
  • Structural checks for equipment foundations, platforms and pipe supports.

Choosing equipment: what we compare besides price

The cheapest offer is rarely the cheapest machine to own. When we compare suppliers we look at the whole picture:

  • Efficiency across the hours the equipment will really run, not only at its rated point.
  • Availability of spare parts and qualified technicians in Albania or the region.
  • Delivery time and installation conditions, including cranes, access and downtime.
  • Noise, vibration, footprint and maintenance access.
  • Warranty terms and what they exclude.
  • Total cost over five to ten years, including energy and maintenance.

Energy efficiency without a complete rebuild

Most plants can reduce energy use without replacing everything. Typical measures include repairing compressed-air leaks, insulating hot and cold pipes, fitting variable-speed drives to pumps and fans, recovering heat from exhaust air, and replacing only the equipment that is clearly oversized or at the end of its life. We start with an audit: we measure the consumption of the main systems over a representative period, rank the measures by payback and propose them in that order. The electrical side of the same project is covered by our electrical engineering service.

What we need from you to start

  • A short description of the product or process and the output you target.
  • Building plans, or photos and key dimensions of the site.
  • A list of existing equipment with nameplate data where available.
  • Energy and utility bills for the last twelve months, if you have them.
  • Your deadline and an approximate budget range.

Mistakes we help you avoid

  • Buying equipment before a utilities study shows whether the site can supply it.
  • Copying a design from another country without adapting it to local climate, power quality and available service.
  • Forgetting maintenance access, so that every filter change needs scaffolding.
  • Skipping commissioning tests, so that problems appear only after the contractor has left.

If the project is part of a larger investment, a business plan built on realistic engineering costs will be far more convincing to a bank than one built on estimates.

Frequently asked questions

How long does a mechanical project take?

It depends on the size. A small line usually takes a few weeks, a full industrial site longer. After the technical visit you receive a written timeline.

Do you also carry out the works?

We supervise execution and can introduce verified contractors. The choice of contractor stays yours, with a clear comparison of offers.

Can you work with existing equipment?

Yes. We audit the current condition, identify weak points and propose improvements without unnecessary intervention.

Do you design heating, ventilation and cooling for buildings as well as for factories?

Yes. We design HVAC for production halls, warehouses, offices, hotels and other commercial buildings, from load calculation to equipment selection and supervision.

Can you review a supplier's quotation before I sign?

Yes. We check whether the offered equipment matches the calculated need, whether anything is missing from the scope, and how the offer compares with others on lifetime cost.

Which standards do you design to?

We work to recognised European and international engineering standards and to the requirements that apply to your project in Albania. We confirm the applicable set with you at the start of each project.

Can you help if our drawings are missing?

Yes. We survey the installation, produce as-built drawings and then use them as the basis for any upgrade.
